## 15.1 — How are critical subprocessors assessed before onboarding?

Security and Privacy assess critical subprocessors for data access, hosting location, independent assurance and incident handling before production use. A named service owner accepts residual risk, and critical suppliers are reassessed annually or after a material incident.

## 15.2 — How are customers notified of new subprocessors?

The subprocessor register identifies the service purpose, location and data categories. Subscribed enterprise contacts receive at least 30 days' advance notice of a planned addition, subject to the standard data processing terms. Objections are handled through the privacy review process.

## 15.3 — Can infrastructure providers access customer content?

The hosting provider operates the underlying infrastructure and may have access under its controlled operational procedures. MeridianDesk restricts application-level support access but does not claim that cloud-provider personnel are technically incapable of all access to stored content.

## 15.4 — Are support vendors permitted to download customer attachments?

External support vendors do not receive production tenant credentials or attachment downloads by default. Any exceptional case requires customer authorization, a scoped support ticket and Privacy approval; anonymized diagnostic information is preferred.

## 15.5 — What happens when a critical supplier is replaced?

The service owner documents data migration, access revocation, credential rotation and deletion confirmation. Security verifies the exit checklist before closing the supplier record. A supplier replacement that changes data processing is also assessed for customer notice obligations.
